## Two things playtesting changed

**Crafting tables stopped being a place to stand and stare.** The real culprit was a craft that had
nothing left to do. FoodChain asks for bread as "how many more to make", but the crafting task reads
its count as an absolute inventory target: with 20 bread already in the pack and a request for 12
more, the task considered itself satisfied before it started, skipped every recipe, and sat at the
open crafting table doing nothing. A never-incremented internal counter meant it could not finish
either. Both are fixed: the craft finishes as soon as its output count is in the inventory, and
FoodChain now asks for "current + needed". A reproduction in a copy of the operator's own world
(20 bread, 36 wheat, the village table) crafted the 12 bread in about 25 seconds, closed the table
and moved on to gathering hay. The earlier two fixes — the recipe-book click being re-sent every
tick, and walking away with the table still open — stay in as well.

**Combat is aimed now, not automatic.** The force field used to swing at anything within 40 degrees
of the crosshair, which in practice looked exactly like killaura — the bot hit mobs it was not
looking at, and because Minecraft resolves a hit from the crosshair it also missed a lot. Every
swing now goes through the vanilla crosshair pick: the mob has to be under the crosshair, the way
it works when you click yourself. Defence is still automatic (it will turn, aim and swing while
you mine), but it turns and aims first. In the combat check, 116 swings were held back for being
off-crosshair and 5 went out; fights are slower than the old force field, and that is the trade the
build is making.

## ⚠️ Read this first — Baritone is REQUIRED, and it is INCLUDED

**There is nothing else to download.** Baritone is the movement engine — pathing, mining and
collecting dropped items — and it **ships inside this jar** as a Fabric nested JAR. UnionClef
declares it under `depends` (`baritone >=1.19.0`), so the requirement is enforced rather than
assumed.

1. Drop **`unionclef-1.0.0+26.2.jar`** into `mods/`.
2. Launch with **Java 25**.

That is the whole install. On first launch the log should show:

```
Baritone bridge ready.
Baritone settings applied: freeLook=false smoothLook=true ...
```

The second line names every tuned value actually set, and flags any this Baritone build refused —
so "the optimal defaults are applied" is something you can check, not assume.

**Already have your own Baritone?** Keep it. Fabric Loader resolves one version per mod id, so a
copy in `mods/` takes priority over the bundled one.

Bundled: `baritone-api-fabric-1.19.0.jar` (LGPL-3.0, unmodified) — the Baritone release built for
26.2. Its licence travels with the jar at `META-INF/licenses/LGPL-3.0.txt`.

## What differs from the 26.3 build

Nothing in behaviour was forked. These are the 26.2 adaptations, all verified by compiling against
the 26.2 client jar:

- **Baritone 1.19.0** is bundled instead of 1.20.0. The bridge's reflection surface was checked
  against both: same settings fields, same process/goal classes, same `IMineProcess` /
  `IFollowProcess` methods.
- **Fuel times come from `Level.fuelValues()`.** 26.3 moved burn times onto a `cooking_fuel` item
  component with a `CONTEXT_INT_PROVIDER` registry; neither exists in 26.2. The 26.2 build asks the
  world's `FuelValues` (the data-map replacement for the old static fuel table), so datapack and
  modded fuels are still correct. With no world loaded yet, it falls back to vanilla burn times.
  `isFuel`, `getFuelAmount` and the smelting chain behave as before.
- **API spellings that changed between 26.2 and 26.3:** `EnderMan` (26.3: `Enderman`),
  `InputConstants.Type.KEYSYM` (26.3: `KEYBOARD`), `InputConstants.isKeyDown(Window, int)`,
  `VanillaRegistries.createLookup()`, `SignBlockEntity#getText(boolean)`,
  `SignText#getMessages(boolean)` returning `Component[]`, and the two-argument
  `LivingEntity#swing(InteractionHand, boolean)` (26.3 added a `SwingAnimation` overload).
- **Metadata:** `minecraft ~26.2`, `cloth-config >=26.2`, `baritone >=1.19.0`.

Everything else — the Baritone-driven movement, humanised aiming, door opening, combat facing,
chain registry, GUI, bridges and shutdown watchdog — is the 1.0.0 work described in the
[26.3 release notes](https://github.com/smallduuude/unionclef-26.3-native/blob/main/docs/releases/1.0.0.md).

## Coverage and known gaps

The item-coverage audit is measured against the 26.2 jar for this build: **1082 of 1098
survival-relevant items are chain-covered (98.5%)**, with 16 documented gaps. The 26.2 build ships all
four new coverage chain families:

- **Structure loot** — a cheat-free singleplayer structure locator (integrated server; no
  command/op) plus a generic loot search for chests, non-ominous trial-chamber vaults and decorated
  pots. Routes the nautilus-armour set, echo shard, trial keys, goat horn, horse armours, dungeon
  discs and the bastion pair. `@locate_structure <id> [go]` is the manual form.
- **Archaeology** — 20 pottery sherds + the relic disc, plus armadillo scute.
- **Mob buckets** — cod/salmon/pufferfish/tropical fish/tadpole/axolotl/sulfur cube and powder snow.
- **Cartographer trades** — the globe banner pattern and every map item this registry version has
  (the 16 structure maps are a 26.3 addition and do not exist on 26.2).

The ominous-trial-chamber family is covered too: `OminousKeyTask` drinks an ominous bottle at a
trial spawner, clears the wave and takes the ejected `ominous_trial_key`, then the ominous vaults
give `heavy_core` (the mace ingredient), `flow_banner_pattern` and `music_disc_creator`.

Remaining gaps, each with the mechanic it needs: the ten skeleton-killed-creeper discs,
husbandry/time items (`blue_egg`, `brown_egg`, `pitcher_pod`, `turtle_scute`), and `elytra` /
`dragon_breath`. See the coverage document
([`docs/features/ITEM_BLOCK_COVERAGE.md`](https://github.com/smallduuude/unionclef-26.3-native/blob/26.2/docs/features/ITEM_BLOCK_COVERAGE.md))
for the measured tables and [`TODOS.md`](https://github.com/smallduuude/unionclef-26.3-native/blob/26.2/TODOS.md) for the outstanding work.
