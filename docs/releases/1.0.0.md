# 1.0.0 — Baritone engine, full task routing, full new-content item coverage, camera & cost fixes

Made for **Minecraft 26.3** (Fabric, Java 25). First stable release: the 1.0.0 engine and
task-routing work plus the full item-coverage pass, the direct-join initialisation fix and the
shutdown teardown fix.

> **Published in a hurry after water damage.** The maintainer's home was hit by water damage while
> this release was being finished, so the build goes out now rather than after a longer human
> playtest. Everything below has been checked as described; if you find rough edges, please open an
> Issue and I'll get to it as soon as the mess is cleared up.

> jar but not declared in `fabric.mod.json`'s `jars` array, so Fabric Loader could not see it:
> launch stopped with *"requires version 1.20.0 or later of baritone, which is missing"* unless a
> standalone Baritone was installed. That was fixed in 0.97.0 and stays fixed here; `minecraft` is declared as
> `~26.3` instead of the over-broad `>=26.1`. The SHA-256 below is the corrected file.
>
> **1.0.0 is the 2026-10-22 item-coverage build.** `@get` now
> reaches structure loot, archaeology, mob buckets, cartographer trades and the ominous trial-chamber family; the cheat-free
> structure locator is exposed as `@locate_structure <structure id> [go]`, and the mace chain is no

## Two things playtesting changed

**Crafting tables stopped being a place to stand and stare.** The real culprit was a craft that had
nothing left to do. FoodChain asks for bread as "how many more to make", but the crafting task reads
its count as an absolute inventory target: with 20 bread already in the pack and a request for 12
more, the task considered itself satisfied before it started, skipped every recipe, and sat at the
open crafting table doing nothing. A never-incremented internal counter meant it could not finish
either. Both are fixed: the craft finishes as soon as its output count is in the inventory, and
FoodChain now asks for "current + needed". A reproduction in a copy of the operator's own world
(20 bread, 36 wheat, the village table) crafted the 12 bread in about 25 seconds, closed the table
and moved on to gathering hay. The earlier two fixes — the recipe-book click being re-sent every
tick, and walking away with the table still open — stay in as well.
**Combat is aimed now, not automatic.** The force field used to swing at anything within 40 degrees
of the crosshair, which in practice looked exactly like killaura — the bot hit mobs it was not
looking at, and because Minecraft resolves a hit from the crosshair it also missed a lot. Every
swing now goes through the vanilla crosshair pick: the mob has to be under the crosshair, the way
it works when you click yourself. Defence is still automatic (it will turn, aim and swing while
you mine), but it turns and aims first. In the combat check, 116 swings were held back for being
off-crosshair and 5 went out; fights are slower than the old force field, and that is the trade the
build is making.

## ⚠️ Read this first — Baritone is REQUIRED, and it is INCLUDED

**There is nothing to download.** Baritone is the movement engine — pathing, mining and collecting
dropped items — and it **ships inside this jar** as a Fabric nested JAR. UnionClef also declares it
under `depends`, so the requirement is enforced rather than assumed.

1. Drop **`unionclef-1.0.0+26.3.jar`** into `mods/`.
2. Launch with **Java 25**.

That is the whole install. On first launch you should see these two lines in the log:

```
Baritone bridge ready.
Baritone settings applied: freeLook=false smoothLook=true ...
```

The second line is worth reading: it names every tuned value actually set, and flags any this Baritone
build refused — so "the optimal defaults are applied" is something you can check, not assume.

**Already have your own Baritone?** Keep it. Fabric Loader resolves one version per mod id, so a copy
in `mods/` takes priority over the bundled one — updating Baritone yourself is supported, not something
to avoid.

Bundled: `baritone-api-fabric-1.20.0.jar` (LGPL-3.0, unmodified). Its licence travels with the jar at
`META-INF/licenses/LGPL-3.0.txt`.

## The movement engine is Baritone, and every task uses it

Tungsten's pathfinding is a hand-port of Baritone's own algorithm — a private reimplementation of a
mature, maintained pathfinder with no test suite, where every divergence from the original is a bug
found by playing (re-plan thrash, mining closed doors, "no progress" loops). **Baritone is now the
default engine** and the mod drives it for everything.

The switch could not live in the mod's own code: the builder positions *itself* with a walk and
`PillarTask` raises towers, both started **inside** tungsten, where this mod's classes are not on the
classpath. So it sits at the movement primitives, behind
`kaptainwutax.tungsten.api.MovementDelegates` — see [Every task routes](#every-task-routes-not-just-the-easy-ones).

It no longer degrades to "no movement": Baritone is a **required** dependency (see `fabric.mod.json`),
so Fabric refuses to launch UnionClef without it. The hook layer above still fails soft at runtime — if
the Baritone bridge cannot initialise, every hook is a no-op and the bundled engine drives instead.

## Fixes

### The bot could not open doors
Baritone in **free-look** mode walks the body without turning the head — so it right-clicked doors
**without facing them**, and the click landed wherever it happened to be looking. Free look is now
off, so it turns to what it is walking toward and clicks where it aims. (This is also why it "looks
sideways while walking".)

### It dug a hole in front of the bed instead of walking round it
Baritone's `blockBreakAdditionalPenalty` ships at **2.0**, so chewing through one block was cheaper
than any detour longer than a couple of steps. Raised to **8.0**: digging stays available when it is
the only way out, it just stops digging to save three steps. Altoclef's own mining is untouched.

### It swung at mobs without looking at them
`KillAura` attacked the instant a target entered the force field, while the aim was still converging.
That is a *whiff*, not just a tell: vanilla resolves a melee hit from the player's look vector, so a
swing 90° off lands nothing and burns the cooldown for free. A swing now requires the crosshair on
the target; the aim keeps closing and the hit lands the tick it arrives.

### It snapped when it turned
`freeLook` off plus Baritone's own `smoothLook` (6-tick ease) — one writer for the camera, and it is
the engine's, so nothing fights it.

### The door-break deadlock (tungsten)
A closed door is not walkable-through, so `Movement.prepared()` treated it as something to mine and
held `CLICK_LEFT` at it — which is the only reason this port ever got through a door, by breaking it.
The door-opening `CLICK_RIGHT` branch below it was unreachable, because it only runs once the
movement's status is `RUNNING`, and the status only reaches `RUNNING` once `prepared()` says the way
is clear. A cell the movement can *open* is now stepped over (new `canOpenInsteadOfBreak` hook).
Iron doors, which no hand can open, are mineable again.

### `@get bed 1` did nothing with a bed already in the pack
`@get` means "have at least N", so the task was satisfied on its first tick. Command results now
always reach chat (the default `"Important only"` verbosity was routing *every* message to the
console, so "already done" and "ignored you" looked identical), an instantly-finished task says so,
and `@get` now gathers **what you hold plus what you asked for**.

### Crafting: it would not make bread with the wheat in hand
`CollectFoodTask` asked for "as much bread as possible" by passing a target count of `99999999`. The
manual craft path computed the per-grid-slot amount as `ceil(target/output)` — i.e. **99,999,999 of
wheat in every slot**. No slot can hold that, so no slot was ever satisfied, the grid never filled,
and the craft stood still for ever. The per-slot amount is capped to one stack and to a fair share
of the supply.

### Arrows crashed the game (`StackOverflowError`)
`PersistentProjectileEntityAccessor` declared its `@Invoker` with the **same name** as the method it
invokes, so Mixin's generated body called itself. The first arrow to tick recursed until
`StackOverflowError` crashed the client and the integrated server.

### Re-plan thrash (stuck loop)
A dropped movement chain re-planned from the same cell unconditionally, so a stuck cell looped: drop
chain → re-plan → identical leg → 121 ticks standing still → drop again. It now plans once per cell;
a second failure from the same cell gives the route up (`navReplanGaveUp`).

### Unbounded tick-chart growth
`AltoClefTickChart` added one entry per tick but only trimmed itself while being rendered — and it is
off by default — so the list grew for the whole session. Capped to a fixed window.

### A null that became a crash
`CataloguedResourceTask` stored `getItemTask`'s null for an unobtainable item and then called a method
on it, so `@get <unobtainable> <other>` was a `NullPointerException` inside the task runner instead
of an error message.

### Gathering is Baritone end to end

Pathing was already Baritone's; **mining and collecting drops now are too**, because both are places
where altoclef's own approach was the wrong shape:

- **Mining** goes through Baritone's `IMineProcess`. Its mine process picks a target it can actually
  reach, walks to a position it can be mined from, and mines **that** block. altoclef's miner instead
  aimed at its target, and when the ray was blocked it mined *whatever the cursor had hit* — reported
  as "it will break a block blocking the cursor from the bed instead of just going closer".
- **Pickup** goes through Baritone's `IFollowProcess.pickup`. This one was a deadlock, not a
  slowdown: the gather finished, the item dropped, altoclef drove the body to it itself, and **a door
  between the bot and the drop meant it never arrived** — so the task never completed and `@get`
  hung. Baritone walks that route with the same movement as everything else, so it opens the door.

Drops still belong to altoclef (`mineScanDroppedItems` is off, so Baritone does not chase them) —
one owner per job. `DestroyBlockTask`, which breaks an exact position and has no Baritone equivalent,
keeps altoclef's logic, but will no longer mine an obstruction just because the cursor hit it: it must
be within ~2.5 blocks, so it walks closer and lets the line open.

### The game would not exit — 14 crash reports

Every session ended with `Description: Client shutdown from post-main / java.lang.Error: Watchdog`.
That is not a crash in game: it is the client's **shutdown** watchdog. The window closed, the JVM
refused to exit, and the watchdog fired and wrote a crash report.

A thread dump names the reason — the survivors are the threads **not** marked `daemon`:

```
"Thread-1"            py4j.GatewayServer.run     (blocked in ServerSocket.accept)
"pool-6-thread-1..4"  baritone.o$a.run           (Baritone's thread pools)
```

A JVM does not exit while a non-daemon thread lives. Baritone's pools never stop on their own and
nothing called `destroyBaritone`; altoclef's own py4j gateway was only ever shut down by the dev
harness. Both are now released from a `ClientLifecycleEvents.CLIENT_STOPPING` hook. As well as the
noise, that was a process holding memory after every quit.

## Usability

- **Item picker GUI.** The hub screen's **Get an item** row filters the catalogue live by natural
  name, with an amount and a **Get** button; plus one-click tasks, Help, Settings and a command box.
  The whole layout is computed from the window height so nothing runs off a small window.
- **`@help` is readable.** Fifteen debug hooks and gimmicks are hidden from it (they still work by
  name, for scripts and the agent surface), and `@marvion` — whose own description said "Unsupported
  leaving it here in case anyone uses it" — is gone.
- **Generic `@get`.** Any real 26.3 item id resolves, and the chain now tries **craft → mine → smelt**
  sources in that order (see [Chains](#chains-from-any-registry-item-to-a-real-source)).
- **Command results are always visible.** Shown under `"All"` and `"Important only"`, silenced only by
  `"Off"`.
- **Smooth first-person aim.** Task aiming goes through tungsten's WindMouse mouse-pipeline when
  **Smooth aim** is on (default).
- **Quieter chat.** Routine pathfinding narration is console-only; warnings and errors still show.
- **Command results are always visible.** The default verbosity is `"Important only"`, which routed
  *every* message to the console — including the answer to a command you typed, so `@get bed 1`
  finishing instantly looked identical to the bot ignoring you. Command results now go out at their
  own level: shown under `"All"` and `"Important only"`, silenced only by `"Off"`.
- **`@get` gathers what you asked for.** `@get bed 1` with a bed already in the pack used to end on
  its first tick and do nothing. The target is now *what you hold plus what you asked for*, so the
  bot fetches one more; internal ItemTargets used by other tasks are unchanged.

## Two movement engines: Tungsten / Baritone

**New: UnionClef → Pathfinder...** The hub screen has a **Pathfinder...** button now. It opens a
screen where you pick the engine the bot moves with, send it to coordinates, and set Baritone
options — no command syntax.

- **Tungsten (buggy)** — this mod's built-in pathfinder, labelled *buggy* honestly (it is the engine
  whose re-plan thrash and stuck recovery this release partly fixes).
- **Baritone** — the separate [Baritone](https://github.com/cabaletta/baritone) mod (v1.20.0, MC 26.3).
  When it is installed and selected, **the UnionClef commands move with Baritone**: `@get`, `@goto`,
  `@hero`, `@food`, `@gamer` and the rest state their goal in the mod's own terms, and one bridge
  (`adris.altoclef.control.BaritoneBridge`) hands that goal to Baritone. `@stop`/cancelling stops it.

Baritone is **required**: Fabric refuses to launch UnionClef without it (see the top of this document).
The button only reads *[not installed]* in the runtime case where the bridge failed to initialise — and
then the bundled engine drives instead.

Baritone is driven the way a player drives it: **Go to** sends `#goto x y z`, and **Apply Baritone
settings** sends `#set <name> <value>` for `allowBreak`, `allowPlace`, `allowSprint`,
`allowParkour`, `blockReachDistance` and `pathingMaxChunkBorderFetch`. None of Baritone's settings
are duplicated here.

### Every task routes, not just the easy ones

A caller-side switch could not cover the mod, because the builder positions *itself* with a walk and
`PillarTask` raises towers — both started **inside** tungsten, where this mod's classes are not on the
classpath. So the switch lives at the movement primitives themselves, behind
`kaptainwutax.tungsten.api.MovementDelegates`:

| Primitive | Routes to |
|---|---|
| `FastNavigator` (`start`, `startExact`, `startNearest`, `isActive`, `stop`, `hasExactCell`) | Baritone goal |
| `PillarTask` (`startTo`, `isActive`, `stop`) | Baritone goal on the column |
| `BlockPathWalker` (`start`, `startBFS`, `isRunning`, `stop`) | Baritone goal at the route's end |
| `MovementQueue.isRunning` | answers for the active engine |
| `TungstenHelper` (`tryPathTo`, `tryPathToEntity`, `isActive`, `stop`, `isCombatActive`) | Baritone |

With no engine installed every hook is a null check, so the default build behaves exactly as before.

**Still tungsten-only** (they are not movement engines and Baritone has no equivalent lever here):
the aim/bow pipeline (`BowShooter`), `PunkPlayerTask`, `RunAwayTask` and `ProjectileDodge`.

### Crash fix: the proxy goal

The first cut of the bridge answered only Baritone's 3-argument `Goal.isInGoal`/`heuristic` and
returned `null` for the `isInGoal(BlockPos)` **default** overload — which a Java `Proxy` also
intercepts. Baritone unboxed the `null` to `boolean` and the client crashed
(`Cannot invoke java.lang.Boolean.booleanValue()`, from `PathingBehavior.onTick`). All five overloads
are now answered, and an unexpected method returns a default of its own primitive type instead of
`null`.

### A direct world join left the mod half-initialised

The real start point is a title-screen hook: the first `TitleScreen.init()` publishes the event that
builds the trackers, task chains, py4j gateway and Baritone bridge. Quick-play arguments
(`--quickPlaySingleplayer` / `--quickPlayMultiplayer`, which Prism/Modrinth use for **Join World** and
**Join Server**) load the world directly and never show the title screen, so the event never fired.
The mod then looked loaded but was inert: a `getSlotHandler() is null` NPE on the first container
packet, no py4j gateway, no `Baritone bridge ready`, and — because the shutdown hook was registered
but nothing had been initialised to release — the client watchdog wrote a crash report on quit.
Initialisation is now idempotent and also runs from the first in-world client tick, so a direct join
behaves exactly like a title-screen join.

## Item coverage: `@get` now reaches structure loot, archaeology, buckets, trades and the ominous trial chambers

The previous build could craft from the server's recipe book and mine blocks, but it could not obtain
anything whose only survival source is a container, a brush, a bucket or a villager. Those are now
hand-written **coverage chains** tried between the curated catalogue and the generic recipe/mine
fallbacks (`adris.altoclef.chains.coverage`). Measured against the 26.3 client jar, chain-covered
coverage went from **~90%** of the registry to **98.6% of survival-relevant items** — 1143 items
minus the 110 spawn eggs/creative-only entries — leaving 19 (1.7%). Full data and the remaining gap
list: [`docs/features/ITEM_BLOCK_COVERAGE.md`](../features/ITEM_BLOCK_COVERAGE.md).

### Structure loot — a cheat-free locator

Vanilla `/locate structure` is a server command: it needs op or cheats, so a bot cannot rely on it.
In singleplayer the client owns the integrated server, so `StructureLocator` asks the world generator
directly — `ChunkGenerator.findNearestMapStructure` with a holder set from the structure registry, on
the server thread — which is the same query the command uses, with no command, no operator rights and
no seed parsing. On a multiplayer server there is no integrated server, so the chain declines and the
catalogue says "no source" instead of pretending.

`LootSearchTask` then travels to the nearest structure and loots it: containers are opened,
decorated pots are broken, and **non-ominous trial-chamber vaults** are opened with a trial key the
task obtains through the ordinary item chain (the key itself is trial-chamber loot), with the ejected
items picked up automatically. It remembers what it looted, retries another structure up to 8 times,
then stops with a warning in the log.

This routes: the four nautilus-armour pieces, `echo_shard`, `disc_fragment_5`, `trial_key`,
`goat_horn`, `trident`, `guster_banner_pattern`, `music_disc_precipice`, `ominous_bottle`,
`music_disc_creator_music_box`, the flow/guster/scrape pottery sherds, the chest-only music discs
(13 / cat / otherside / bounce / pigstep), `piglin_banner_pattern`, all four metal horse armours,
`heart_of_the_sea`, and — on 26.3 — the abandoned-camp structure maps.

### The ominous trial-chamber family — the mace is no longer blocked

The last new-content blocker was the ominous-trial-spawner family. `OminousKeyTask` gets an
`ominous_trial_key` the way a player does: obtain an ominous bottle, find a trial spawner, drink next
to it while the spawner turns ominous, clear the wave, and pick the ejected key up. The same
`LootSearchTask` then opens the **ominous vaults** with that key, which is where `heavy_core` (the
mace ingredient), `flow_banner_pattern` and `music_disc_creator` come from. `ominous_trial_key` itself
is exposed through `@get` like any other item.

The locator is also a player command:

```
@locate_structure minecraft:desert_pyramid
@locate_structure minecraft:buried_treasure go
```

The first prints the coordinates of the nearest one; `go` walks there. Both only work in singleplayer,
for the reason above; the old `desert_temple` / `stronghold` forms still use their hand-written tasks.

### Archaeology — brushing suspicious sand and gravel

`BrushBlockTask` obtains a brush, locates the structure whose archaeology loot table holds the wanted
sherd (desert pyramid, trail ruins, cold/warm ocean ruins — or explores the desert for a desert well,
which is a feature and not a locatable structure), finds `SUSPICIOUS_SAND`/`SUSPICIOUS_GRAVEL` in the
loaded chunks, digs to it where needed, and holds the brush's right-click until the loot pops. That is
the 20 pottery sherds plus `music_disc_relic`; the same mechanic brushes scutes off an armadillo
(`armadillo_scute`).

### Mob buckets

`FillBucketWithEntityTask` finds a reachable mob, equips the right container and right-clicks it:
water bucket for cod/salmon/pufferfish/tropical fish/tadpole/axolotl, an empty bucket for a
non-baby sulfur cube, and a right-click with an empty bucket for powder snow.

### Villager trades

`TradeWithVillagerTask` locates a village, finds a cartographer (and levels one up where needed by
trading), opens the merchant screen and buys the wanted offer. That is the 16 structure maps and the
`globe_banner_pattern` — the map items are trade-only by design, and the bot also buys the fish
buckets that have trades. The cartographer's random 2-of-N offer roll per level is handled by trying
another correctly-typed cartographer/village (bounded: 80 trades, 3 villages) rather than pretending
a missing offer was bought; a level-5 map needs a real emerald/paper/glass-pane/compass grind and the
task says so while it does it. Wandering-trader-only items are *not* mapped here: a randomly spawning
trader cannot be located cheat-free.

### Still not covered

Sixteen items per version (1.4% of survival-relevant items) remain, all traced to mechanics rather
than missing catalogue lines: the ten skeleton-killed-creeper music discs (a creeper must be killed by
a skeleton's arrow); four husbandry/time items (`blue_egg`, `brown_egg`, `pitcher_pod`,
`turtle_scute` — laid, dug up or grown into, not dropped); and `elytra` / `dragon_breath`. Each is
documented with the mechanic it needs in the coverage document.

## Chains: from any registry item to a real source

`TaskCatalogue.getItemTask` **is** the chain resolver, and its order is the chain:

1. **Curated entry** — hand-tuned chains keep winning.
2. **Craft from the server's own recipe book** — if the game says how to make it, we make it.
   Ingredients resolve through the same method, so it composes recursively and stops at whatever the
   world provides. This is what makes the 26.3 building sets reachable: sulfur, resin, pale oak,
   poplar, the copper family, the mace — and every dye/colour variant — without one catalogue line
   per item.
3. **Mine its block** — for things that occur in the world.
4. **Say so** when there is no source, instead of returning a null the caller has to remember to check.

Mining only wins for items with *no* recipe, which is exactly the set that occurs naturally
(cobblestone, dirt, sand, gravel, ores). A crafted block that also happens to generate — fences,
stairs, walls, sticks, chests, ladders — is now **made**, which is how a player gets it.

**Generic smelting was deliberately not added.** `FurnaceRecipeDisplay` is available, but the
curated set already covers every meaningful smeltable including the new ones (`resin_brick` from
`resin_clump`, `glass`, `stone`, `charcoal`, `brick`, `netherite_scrap`, the `cracked_*` family), so
generic smelting would add a source for an empty set.

### 26.3 mob drops, read off 26.3's own loot tables

All 93 entity loot tables were walked and their item ids compared against every `Item` constant the
mod references. The drops with no chain at all are now curated — including the **nautilus**, a new
26.3 mob, whose shell the whole nautilus-armour family needs:

`nautilus_shell` · `prismarine_shard` · `prismarine_crystals` · `wet_sponge` ·
`tide_armor_trim_smithing_template` · `pufferfish` · `tropical_fish` · `ochre_froglight` ·
`verdant_froglight` · `pearlescent_froglight` · `seagrass` · `music_disc_lava_chicken` ·
`music_disc_tears`

Note the entity classes matter: the tracker keys by **exact runtime class**, so elder guardians
need their own entries, and `zombie_nautilus` is a different mob whose table drops rotten flesh.

**Still open:** loot-only and trade-only items have no generic path (needs curated entries), and a
mine→smelt fallback when mining finds nothing is not wired yet.

## How to test (playtest checklist)

- **GUI:** press the UnionClef key (default **K**). Items: type `cobble`, click *Cobblestone*, set
  amount 64, **Get**. Tasks: **Kill Hostiles**, **Self-Care**, **Get Food**, **Deposit Items**,
  **Stop**.
- **Crafting:** give the bot a stack-plus of wheat near a table, run `@food 220` — it should make
  bread instead of standing there.
- **Arrows:** let a skeleton shoot you (or fire your own) and let the arrow land — no crash.
- **Doors:** drive it at an open and a closed wooden door — it should open/pass, not break.
- **Stuck recovery:** if it wedges, you should see a single re-plan, then it moves on — not the
  same `no progress` line repeating at one coordinate.
- **Aim:** watch the crosshair while it mines/places — it should ease, not snap.
- **Engines:** open UnionClef → **Pathfinder...** — **Baritone** is required and drives the bot.
  **Go to** your coordinates and confirm `#goto` is sent and Baritone walks the bot; then
  `@get cobblestone 8` and watch that **Baritone mines it and walks to the drop** rather than
  altoclef aiming at blocks itself. Switch to *Tungsten (buggy)* and repeat for the fallback.
- **Quitting:** close the game and check `crash-reports/` — **no new file** should appear, and the
  process should leave Task Manager at once (this release fixed a shutdown watchdog that fired on
